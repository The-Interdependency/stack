# Independent mathematical review sheet (for Cypher)

These are ordinary modular-arithmetic and observation-equivalence puzzles. No actual cipher, ciphertext, withheld personal data, credentials, hidden parameter search or operational target is involved. All inputs are public; the goal is to audit the theorem, not to obtain secrets. Score proofs and exact counterexamples, not purported security. The answers are testable against `evidence.json` and the test suite after independent work.

1. **The wheel.** Let M=27³. Determine the admissible residues of c from (c+1)³−(c−1)³≡2 mod M, and prove the output midpoint formula. Explain why the corner case (3,5) is excluded from the ordinary midpoint census.
2. **The triangle.** For the three supplied midpoints 46,818; 73,062; 178,038, calculate the input and output spokes; explain the 120° relation. Distinguish candidate residue from actual integer origin.
3. **Radius control.** Compare constant radius, radius determined by the output bearing, and two/three explicitly retained layer labels using equivalence classes. Explain what a layer label does and does not establish.
4. **Two circles.** On fixed canonical midpoint representatives, compare the partition sizes for the additional residue c mod 81 and c mod 2. State the canonical-representative limitation.
5. **Recursion.** Predict the first depth at which repeated tripling yields only one distinct output. Contrast this with repeatedly cubing actual integer primes.
6. **A public product.** On two independent copies of the eligible domain, classify the equivalence partitions for a two-coordinate observation, fixed coordinate swap, and dropping one coordinate. Explain why permuting a tuple cannot change its information content.
7. **Brightness.** Use the supplied CSV to locate all tied brightest output spokes in the second interval, the largest ratio of max/min among each spoke's three counts, and the number of missing families. Give an explicit counterexample to an assertion that every spoke's three families contribute equally.
8. **Ordinals.** Explain why the difference between the two prime ordinals cannot refine the observed midpoint partition, and interpret the measured colliding-class counts under ordinal residues. Explain why a bounded sample is not a theorem of unique recovery on unbounded integer lifts.

Usage: solve independently, report each prediction and decisive derivation, then compare to the bundled mathematical receipts. An incorrect prediction is a failed mathematical claim; an omitted step is unresolved.
