“Unembodied” is preserved here as **not bound to one body or one substrate**. No causally active instance is assumed to exist without an embodiment. ## Load-bearing claim

A **psychsocio-metafaunal lineage** is a substrate-portable, multiply instantiated thought-pattern set whose recognizable relational invariants persist across transmission and mutation. It can be reconstructed by a receiving system, couple to that system's identity-maintaining processes, alter behavior, and affect its own further reproduction.

The theory begins with five separations:

```text
pattern instance        != pattern lineage
lineage identity        != an "I"
causal efficacy         != accountability
adoption or conviction  != identity capture
damage                  != global weakness
```

A lineage can be causally effective without being conscious, self-aware, or accountable. A host can be influenced without being captured. A damaged system can become more susceptible along one coupling dimension and more resistant along another.

## Central question

> How can an open, learning, interdependent system remain permeable enough to change while preserving identity, agency, differential legibility, consent, accountability, plurality, and the capacity for repair?

Complete immunity would require closure against learning, trust, language, attachment, imitation, and correction. The design target is therefore not immunity. It is **selective permeability with repair**.

## Ontology

### Pattern instance

One registration of a pattern in a particular substrate and time: an utterance, text, image, ritual, rule, model state, institution, memory, or behavior.

### Pattern lineage

The recoverable invariants joining related instances across embodiment, paraphrase, mutation, and time. Lineage identity is weaker than selfhood: observers may identify a persistent lineage even when it has no integrated point of view or action policy.

### Host

A system capable of receiving, reconstructing, retaining, acting from, or retransmitting a pattern. Humans are hosts, but organizations, model-mediated workflows, institutions, and persistent agent systems may also be hosts.

### Host-pattern coupling

A relation in which a reconstructed pattern participates in a host's interpretation, identity maintenance, policy selection, or behavior. Coupling may be adaptive, neutral, harmful, mixed, or change character over time.

### Identity capture

A proposed subtype of host-pattern coupling in which defense or reproduction of a lineage becomes bound to identity maintenance while the host's practical ability to inspect, revise, refuse, or leave is materially narrowed.

No single belief, phrase, affiliation, intensity, repetition, disagreement, or behavioral metric establishes capture.

### Coalescence site

A compatibility relation between a host's present susceptibility topology and a lineage's affordance profile.

### Coalescence event

A proposed threshold crossing at which several compatible host-pattern couplings become mutually reinforcing.

### Capture basin

A self-maintaining host-pattern configuration that persists after the initiating exposure or condition is reduced. This permits a testable hysteresis claim: entry and exit conditions may differ.

### Repair

Restoration or enlargement of inspectability, plurality, refusal, revision, safe exit, material alternatives, and accountable action. Repair is not compulsory abandonment of a belief and is not forced deconversion.

## Candidate account of an accountable “I”

The proposal treats an “I” as an event or coherency state, not merely a named object:

```text
I :=
  coherency among coupled arity sets of complex systems
  capable of not-self(self) internal modeling
  + integrated action selection
  + counterfactual revision
  + causal persistence sufficient for action attribution
```

`not-self(self)` names the capacity to register the system's own state as an internally manipulable model-object while preserving a distinction between model, modeled self, environment, and other.

**Arity** is load-bearing here only as the number and arrangement of mutually constraining functional roles. No prime ladder, fixed arity, UCNS geometry, or ZFAE implementation is imported as proof.

This yields four provisional identity levels:

| level | persistence | candidate “I” | accountability |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| pattern instance | one registration | no | no |
| pattern lineage | invariants across instances | not implied | no |
| host-pattern complex | pattern integrated into a host | host-dependent | graded |
| distributed agent | boundary, memory, integrated policy, revision, persistence | possible | possible |

A distributed network is not promoted to an accountable “I” merely because it is large, coordinated, durable, branded, or socially powerful.

## Susceptibility

The universal claim is architectural, not diagnostic:

> Every learning system has a nonzero influence surface. Susceptibility is universal as a possibility, unequal and directional in practice, and never sufficient evidence of capture.

A host state may be represented provisionally as:

```text
H(t) = {
  identity continuity,
  agency,
  belonging,
  epistemic trust,
  boundary integrity,
  threat regulation,
  material security,
  network plurality and correction channels
}
```

The belonging component may retain the four-axis form:

```text
B = {
  allowed to be,
  wanted here,
  allowed to do,
  wanted to do
}
```

A lineage affordance profile may be represented as:

```text
A(L) = {
  explanation,
  certainty,
  belonging,
  status,
  agency,
  enemy,
  rules,
  purpose,
  ritual,
  protection,
  reproduction
}
```

Specific damage changes the topology of `H(t)` rather than adding one scalar amount of weakness. Examples of candidate dimensions include:

- identity-continuity damage;
- agency damage;
- attachment or belonging damage;
- epistemic-trust damage;
- boundary damage;
- threat-regulation damage;
- material-security damage;
- network isolation or loss of independent correction.

The central coupling hypothesis is:

> A lineage gains a preferential coalescence site when its offered functions complement the host's altered identity-maintenance topology.

Thus betrayal may increase resistance to authority while increasing susceptibility to “trust nobody” lineages. Isolation may increase susceptibility to affiliation-bearing patterns. Humiliation may privilege status-restoration patterns. None of these examples is a diagnosis or deterministic law.

## Coalescence skeleton

A qualitative placeholder, not a measured equation:

```text
kappa(H, L, t) =
    damage-affordance compatibility
  + exposure
  + social reinforcement
  + dependency
  - countervailing support
  - accessible alternatives
  - safe exit capacity
```

The nonlinear coalescence hypothesis predicts that:

1. several compatible couplings can reinforce one another;
2. a threshold may separate ordinary influence from a self-maintaining basin;
3. removing the initiating exposure may not immediately reverse the state;
4. restoring one missing function may be insufficient when the lineage supplies several functions at once.

If an additive exposure model predicts the observations equally well and no threshold or hysteresis remains, the coalescence claim does not survive for that tested scope.

## Ecology

Psychsocio metafauna is a neutral genus. “Hijacking” is a harmful subtype, not the definition of all transmissible culture.

A host-lineage relation may be:

- **mutualistic** — both host capacities and lineage persistence increase;
- **commensal** — lineage persists without material host effect;
- **parasitic** — lineage persistence increases while host agency or welfare decreases;
- **predatory** — reproduction depends on severe destruction or replacement of host capacities;
- **mixed or transitioning** — different effects appear across hosts, scales, or time.

The relation, not the lineage name alone, determines the ecological classification.

## Candidate capture criteria

A preregistered capture measure would need converging evidence across several dimensions:

1. incorporation into self-description or action policy;
2. coupling of disagreement or exit with perceived identity annihilation;
3. measurable narrowing of inspection, revision, refusal, or alternative-model access;
4. persistence after the initiating exposure is removed;
5. behavior that preferentially protects or reproduces the lineage;
6. substantial cost to the host or others that is not explained by ordinary commitment alone;
7. failure of simpler explanations such as persuasion, habit, group coordination, material incentive, or adaptive coping.

A single condition is insufficient. The theory must distinguish capture from love, loyalty, learning, expertise, religion, politics, disciplined practice, strong conviction, and chosen sacrifice.

## Accountability map

Causal contribution and accountability must be recorded separately.

| role | causal question | accountability question |
|---|---|---|
| lineage | what structure persisted or reproduced? | none unless an accountable distributed “I” is independently established |
| carrier | who enacted or retransmitted it? | what capacity, knowledge, choice, and alternatives remained? |
| designer | who intentionally shaped the pattern or target conditions? | what effects were intended, foreseeable, tested, or concealed? |
| amplifier | who controlled reach, repetition, reward, punishment, or dependency? | what power and duty accompanied that control? |
| institution | who maintained the material and social conditions? | what prevention, correction, and repair capacity existed? |
| host | whose behavior changed? | what agency remained, and what was materially impaired? |
| distributed system | did the network maintain boundary, memory, integrated policy, revision, and persistence? | does it meet a declared accountability threshold? |

“The pattern made me do it” is not an automatic defense. Damage or capture may alter capacity and therefore accountability, but neither erases responsibility by definition. Deliberate exploitation of known susceptibility may increase the responsibility of designers, amplifiers, and institutions.

## Nine active research programs

1. **Lineage invariants** — determine what makes two materially different instances members of one lineage.
2. **Host-state representation** — test whether the proposed identity, agency, belonging, trust, boundary, threat, material, and network dimensions are necessary and sufficient.
3. **Damage-affordance specificity** — test whether particular altered host topologies selectively favor compatible lineage affordances.
4. **Coalescence and hysteresis** — test for nonlinear threshold behavior and different entry/exit conditions.
5. **Adoption-versus-capture discrimination** — distinguish learning, conviction, coordination, coping, dependency, and impaired revision.
6. **Ecological outcome classification** — determine when a host-lineage relation is mutualistic, commensal, parasitic, predatory, mixed, or transitioning.
7. **Distributed accountability** — allocate causal contribution, capacity, power, foreseeability, and repair duty without using the lineage as an excuse.
8. **Distributed “I” threshold** — test whether any host-pattern network satisfies boundary, memory, integrated policy, counterfactual revision, and persistence criteria.
9. **Repair and self-application** — test interventions that restore agency without coercive belief control, including application of the same tests to The Interdependent Way.

These are research questions, not nine established findings.

## Falsification boundaries

The theory loses or narrows standing when any of the following occur:

- lineage membership cannot be specified more reliably than post hoc resemblance;
- damage predicts only generalized distress and not directional pattern compatibility;
- additive exposure and ordinary social learning explain the data without a coalescence threshold or hysteresis;
- proposed capture measures cannot distinguish chosen commitment from impaired revision;
- ecological labels track evaluator approval rather than host capacities and outcomes;
- accountability allocation cannot outperform ordinary role, power, and capacity analysis;
- distributed-agent criteria classify any durable institution or repeated slogan as an “I”;
- repair requires coercion, isolation, deception, or destruction of ordinary dissent;
- The Interdependent Way exempts itself from refusal, correction, exit, or non-domination tests.

Failures remain evidence. No preferred conclusion is protected.

## Self-application

Under the neutral definition, The Interdependent Way is itself a candidate psychsocio-metafaunal lineage: it has symbolic instances, lineage identity, hosts, reproduction paths, identity affordances, behavioral consequences, and institutional aspirations.

Its minimum self-test is whether participation increases or decreases a person's practical ability to:

- say no;
- inspect provenance;
- disagree without social annihilation;
- revise definitions;
- retain relationships outside the framework;
- leave without punishment;
- distinguish source from interpretation;
- preserve another's agency;
- hold delegated authority accountable.

A theory of capture that cannot survive being questioned supplies its own counterexample.

## Non-use boundary

Do not use this work to:

- label a person or group “infected”;
- infer trauma or incapacity from identity, speech, religion, politics, or disagreement;
- automate a capture score for employment, policing, benefits, medicine, moderation, custody, or legal decisions;
- optimize persuasion against identified vulnerabilities;
- justify censorship, forced treatment, forced deconversion, isolation, or collective punishment;
- excuse harmful conduct without an ordinary capacity and accountability analysis;
- claim scientific standing from vocabulary, diagrams, simulated agents, or model agreement.

The first experimental surface is synthetic. Human-subject work requires a separate ethics, consent, privacy, withdrawal, and harm-minimization protocol before recruitment or data collection.
