# Hopf Fibration Note

External reference from X post by @0xEronn (2026-11-02). Recorded in stack as stack-local research note against the graduated EPAC project. Canonical copy also lives in The-Interdependency/epac.

Heinz Hopf (1931) discovered the Hopf fibration: a map \( S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \xrightarrow{h} S^2 \) where the fibers are circles that link each other exactly once and fill the 3-sphere without intersecting.

Roger Penrose referred to it as "an element of the architecture of our world." It appears in at least eight areas of physics, including:

- Quantum computing: the Bloch sphere representation of qubit states (up to global phase), where the fiber corresponds to the discarded global phase.
- Gauge theory and fiber bundles.
- Quaternion algebra underlying 3D rotations in robotics, graphics engines, and embedding rotations.
- Potential relevance to geometry in LLM embedding spaces.

The accompanying video (sourced from Dimensions-math.org / Jos Leys) provides stereographic projections and visualizations of the linking fibers, also connecting to magnetic monopoles and knots of pure light.
